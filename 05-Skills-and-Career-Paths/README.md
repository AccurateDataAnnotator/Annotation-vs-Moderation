# Module 05 — Skills and Career Paths

## What You Will Learn

- The core skills needed for annotation work
- The core skills needed for moderation work
- How to position yourself depending on your background
- What a realistic career path looks like in this field

---

## Skills for Data Annotation

These are the skills that annotation projects look for:

**Attention to Detail**
Every label matters. One wrong label affects the model.
You need to slow down and read carefully every time.

**Guideline Comprehension**
Annotation projects come with detailed instruction documents.
Your ability to read, understand, and apply those guidelines
consistently is your most important skill.

**Written Communication**
Many annotation tasks require you to write feedback,
explain your reasoning, or justify your label choices.
Clear writing is essential.

**Patience with Repetition**
You may label hundreds of similar items in one session.
Staying accurate when the work feels repetitive is a real skill.

**Technical Comfort**
You do not need to be a developer. But you need to be comfortable
working inside annotation platforms, following structured formats,
and submitting work correctly.

---

## Skills for Content Moderation

These are the skills that moderation roles look for:

**Policy Understanding**
Every platform has its own rules. You need to learn them
quickly and apply them without personal bias.

**Emotional Resilience**
Moderation often involves reviewing disturbing content.
This is a real part of the job that candidates rarely prepare for.

**Speed and Accuracy Together**
Moderation is often time sensitive. You need to make
accurate decisions quickly without sacrificing quality.

**Consistency Under Pressure**
You may review thousands of items per day.
Your decisions need to stay consistent from item one to item one thousand.

**Escalation Judgment**
Knowing when a case is beyond your level and needs
to go to a specialist is a critical moderation skill.

---

## Skills That Work in Both Fields

- Bilingual or multilingual ability
- Reading comprehension across different writing styles
- Familiarity with AI systems and how they work
- Ability to follow complex written instructions
- Professional communication and reporting

---

## Realistic Career Paths

**Path 01 — Starting with Annotation**
Freelance annotation tasks on platforms like Toloka or DataAnnotation
leads to longer term contracts with companies like Innodata or Appen.
From there you can move into quality assurance or project coordination.

**Path 02 — Starting with Moderation**
Entry level moderation for smaller platforms leads to
trust and safety roles at larger companies.
With experience you can move into policy writing or training new moderators.

**Path 03 — The Hybrid Path**
Building knowledge in both fields positions you for
AI safety roles, RLHF projects, and multilingual evaluation work.
This is the highest demand path right now.

---

## Where to Start if You Are New

Start with annotation. Here is why:

- The guidelines are more structured and easier to learn from
- The feedback loop helps you understand what quality means
- The skills transfer directly into moderation when you are ready
- Platforms are more accessible for beginners globally

---

## Quick Summary

- Annotation requires patience, detail, and guideline consistency
- Moderation requires resilience, speed, and policy judgment
- Both fields reward bilingual candidates significantly
- Starting with annotation is the most practical entry point
