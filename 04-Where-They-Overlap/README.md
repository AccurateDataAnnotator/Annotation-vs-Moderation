## The Overlap Zone

There is a space where annotation and moderation are not opposites.
They are partners.

That space is called AI safety work.

When companies build AI models that interact with real users,
they need humans who can do both:

- Label harmful content to train safety filters
- Review AI outputs to catch policy violations before they go live

That is one job. It requires both skill sets.

---

## Where You Will See This in Real Work

**RLHF Projects:**
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback requires annotators
to rate AI responses for quality, safety, and helpfulness.
This is annotation. But the content being reviewed is often
sensitive, harmful, or policy relevant.
That requires a moderation mindset.

**Trust and Safety Teams:**
Large AI companies have teams that do both.
They label training data for harmful content classifiers
and also review flagged outputs from live AI systems.
Same team. Same person. Both roles.

**Multilingual Safety Review:**
A company building a global AI product needs reviewers
who can read content in multiple languages and decide:
Is this harmful? Should this be used as training data?
That single question sits exactly on the line between the two fields.

---

## The Hybrid Job Listing

Watch for these phrases in job listings:

- "Review and label AI generated content"
- "Evaluate model outputs for safety and quality"
- "Flag policy violations in training datasets"
- "Assess harmful content for AI safety projects"

These are hybrid roles. They pay for both skill sets.
They are also where bilingual candidates have the strongest advantage.
